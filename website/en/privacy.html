Privacy Policy

Effective date: October 19, 2026

Ling Ling Suite ("Ling Ling") is an open-source desktop program maintained by Suikann that runs on your own computer. This policy explains what data Ling Ling touches, where it sends it, where it keeps it, and how to delete it. The Traditional Chinese version is the primary text; if the two differ, the Chinese version prevails.

At a glance

  • Ling Ling has no server. The maintainer receives none of your data, and the program contains no ads, tracking or usage statistics.
  • Google data travels only between your computer and the Google account you sign in with (Drive and Sheets).
  • Sign-in tokens are kept in the operating system's secure storage and are never shared with anyone.
  • For HuaXia you sign in with your own Discord account, and distributed data is written directly from your computer to the club's HuaXia.
  • Log files stay on your computer and contain no tokens, passwords or personal data of HuaXia members.

What Ling Ling connects to

Ling Ling connects to only two places, and only when you use the related features:

  • Google: to sign in, and to read and write the club's catalog (a Google spreadsheet plus Drive).
  • The club's HuaXia (the website of the HuaXia Chinese Orchestra Club, with its data on Supabase): to sign in with Discord and to distribute parts.

When you only use local features such as splitting, rotating, renaming and compressing, Ling Ling does not use the network. Ling Ling never connects to a server run by the maintainer or any other third party. During sign-in, Ling Ling opens a local port on 127.0.0.1 that accepts a single callback; other computers cannot reach it.

Google data

Requested permissions

  • https://www.googleapis.com/auth/drive: see, edit, create and delete files in your Google Drive.
  • https://www.googleapis.com/auth/spreadsheets: see, edit, create and delete your Google Sheets spreadsheets.

Most of the catalog's folders and parts are created and uploaded by hand in the Drive web interface, and Ling Ling needs to list, rename, move and download those files, so it needs the drive permission. If either permission is left unchecked on the consent screen, Ling Ling does not keep the sign-in. Ling Ling recommends signing in with the club's catalog account rather than a personal account.

What Ling Ling uses these permissions for

  • Reading and writing the catalog spreadsheet chosen in Catalog Settings: pieces, editions, movements, instrumentation, headcounts, durations, section mappings, club settings, and the instrumentation write log and archive log.
  • Listing, uploading, downloading, renaming and moving scores and parts in the catalog's folders; when a file is replaced or an archive is reverted, the old file is moved to the Drive trash.
  • Listing the folder names in your Drive while you choose the catalog folder in Catalog Settings.
  • Reading the signed-in account's email address from Drive's account information, to show it in Catalog Settings and pre-fill it at the next sign-in.

Although the drive permission covers your whole Drive, Ling Ling only reads and writes the items listed above.

Records written to the catalog

When it writes instrumentation back or archives files to the catalog, Ling Ling adds rows to the "編制寫入紀錄" (instrumentation write log) and "存入紀錄" (archive log) sheets of the catalog spreadsheet, recording the time and the signed-in account's email address; the archive log also records the file's path on your computer. Anyone who can open the spreadsheet can see these records.

Google data kept on your computer

  • Preferences: the email address of the last sign-in, and the IDs of the catalog spreadsheet and catalog folder.
  • Project files (.llproj, saved where you choose): which piece, edition and movement each group is linked to, and a copy of that place's instrumentation.
  • Files downloaded from the catalog: put back where they were before clearing, or in a folder you choose. Temporary files used during a download are kept in the user data folder and deleted when the download ends.

Sharing and limited use

  • Ling Ling does not send Google data anywhere outside Google, with one exception: when an officer presses "Distribute..." in Ling Ling and confirms with "Publish" in the preview, Ling Ling writes that group's instrumentation (voices, headcounts, sections) and the selected score and parts from your computer to the club's HuaXia (see the next section). This instrumentation may come from the catalog.
  • Ling Ling does not sell or rent Google data, does not use it for advertising, and does not use it to train artificial intelligence or machine learning models. The maintainer cannot see this data.
  • Ling Ling's use and transfer of information received from Google APIs adheres to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements.

Tokens and secure storage

  • After you sign in to Google, Ling Ling keeps only the refresh token, in the operating system's secure storage: Credential Manager on Windows, and the Secret Service (for example GNOME Keyring or KWallet) on Linux. The entry is named LingLingSuite Google; the HuaXia entry is LingLingSuite HuaXia and is stored only when an officer signs in.
  • Access tokens stay only in the running program and are never written to a file.
  • If the computer has no secure storage or it cannot be written to, Ling Ling does not keep the sign-in, shows the reason in Catalog Settings, and asks you to sign in again next time. Ling Ling never falls back to a plain-text file.
  • Tokens are sent only back to the service that issued them (Google or HuaXia) to renew or use them. They are never shared with anyone and never written to the log files.
  • Older versions of Ling Ling stored the Google token in plain text as google_token.json; the current version deletes that file at every startup without reading it.

HuaXia and Discord

  • To distribute to HuaXia, you sign in with your own Discord account. Ling Ling opens the Discord sign-in page in your browser through HuaXia's sign-in service; your Discord password is entered only on Discord's website, and Ling Ling never sees it.
  • After sign-in, Ling Ling receives HuaXia sign-in tokens and your Discord account name. The name is used to show who is signed in and is written to the distribution log (see below); Ling Ling does not store it on your computer.
  • Ling Ling then asks HuaXia whether you are an officer. If you are not, Ling Ling reads the status of your own account (for example awaiting approval or disabled) to explain why, then signs out and keeps no token.
  • Ling Ling reads pieces, voices, scores and this semester's concerts from HuaXia for matching and previews. It does not read the member list or other members' data.
  • After you confirm a distribution, Ling Ling writes directly from your computer to the club's HuaXia: pieces, voices, headcounts, sections, the score and part PDFs, and the concerts they are attached to, without passing through any other server. The writes are made as you and follow HuaXia's own permission rules; non-officers cannot write.
  • Each distribution is also recorded in the catalog spreadsheet's distribution log, with the time, the operator's Discord account name, and the pieces and files distributed.
  • Data written to HuaXia is managed by HuaXia's maintainers under HuaXia's own rules; Discord sign-in is covered by Discord's privacy policy.

Log files and other local files

Ling Ling keeps its local data in the user data folder: %APPDATA%\LingLingSuite\ on Windows, and ~/.config/LingLingSuite/ (or $XDG_CONFIG_HOME/LingLingSuite/) on Linux. These files are never uploaded.

  • Log files (logs/, one file per day, kept for 30 days): catalog read and write failures and important actions (archiving, reverting an archive, clearing local copies, downloading, distributing). Each entry contains only the time, action, piece title, file name, local path, error category and raw error message; anything in the raw message that looks like a token or password is replaced with ***. Log files contain no tokens, passwords or personal data of HuaXia members. Help > Open Log Folder opens the folder.
  • Preferences, undo and redo history, the workspace (split output), PDF rotation backups, the temporary folder and the single-instance lock file.

Deleting data and revoking access

  • Press "Sign Out" in Catalog Settings to delete the Google token from this computer's secure storage. To make Google revoke Ling Ling's access as well, remove Ling Ling Suite under Third-party apps & services in your Google Account.
  • Press "Sign Out of HuaXia" to end this computer's HuaXia session and delete the HuaXia token from secure storage.
  • Sign out first, then delete the user data folder above to remove all data Ling Ling keeps on this computer (except project files and files saved in locations you chose).
  • The catalog spreadsheet, the files in Drive and the data on HuaXia belong to the club's accounts; manage and delete them in Google Drive or HuaXia.

Changes to this policy

When this policy changes, this page and the effective date above are updated; every revision can be found in the change history on GitHub.

Contact

For questions about this policy or how Ling Ling handles data, please open an issue on GitHub Issues.
